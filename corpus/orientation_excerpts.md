# 01-hipaa-for-observers.md

# Kit 01 — HIPAA basics for observers (v0.1.0)

**Audience:** High school, college, and early-career observers  
**Purpose:** Educational primer — not legal advice. Follow your site’s privacy training.

## Core idea

If you can identify a person and learn something about their health or care, treat it as confidential. Do not share it outside the authorized care/education setting.

## Observer rules of thumb

1. **No stories outside the walls** — not to friends, social media, group chats, or class presentations with identifiable details.
2. **No photos or recordings** of patients, screens, whiteboards, labels, or documents unless the site explicitly authorizes a supervised educational activity.
3. **Minimum necessary** — only look/listen to what your host says you may observe for learning.
4. **Ask before accessing anything** — never touch EHR workstations, paper charts, or badge doors without instruction.
5. **Report mistakes immediately** — if you overhear something sensitive or make an error, tell your preceptor/coordinator the same day.

## Identifiers to avoid repeating

Names, faces, room numbers combined with dates, unique stories, school/employer details that pinpoint a person, photos of wristbands, etc.

## Reflection prompt (for journals)

Write what you *learned about a role or workflow* without including who the patient was or unique clinical details.

# 02-site-etiquette.md

# Kit 02 — Site etiquette & professionalism (v0.1.0)

## Before you arrive

- Confirm time, entrance, parking, dress code, and badging requirements in writing.
- Bring government ID if required; arrive early.
- Silence your phone; use it only if your host allows.

## On the floor

- Introduce yourself: name, school/program, that you are an **observer**.
- Stand where you are told; do not block doorways, crash carts, or workstations.
- Do not give clinical advice, translate medical decisions, or touch patients unless your program and site authorize hands-on activities (many shadowing programs are observation-only).
- If a patient or family is uncomfortable, step out immediately and thank them.

## Communication

- Use calm, respectful language.
- Save clinical questions for hallway debriefs, not mid-procedure.
- Never argue with staff in front of patients.

## After the session

- Thank your host.
- Complete any hour-verification steps your program requires.
- Reflect privately without PHI.

# 05-social-media-boundaries.md

# Kit 05 — Social media boundaries (v0.1.0)

## Default rule

**If it happened in a clinical setting, don’t post it** — even if you think it is anonymized. Unique stories are often re-identifiable.

## Never post

- Patient faces, voices, rooms, wristbands, monitors, whiteboards
- Staff gossip or complaints about named clinicians
- “Guess the diagnosis” games from real encounters
- Photos in scrubs that include unit signage + timestamps that locate care

## Safer alternatives

- Post about your career goals, study habits, or public events (HOSA competitions)
- Thank mentors only with their permission and without clinical details
- Share open educational resources and Cross Clinical OSS tools

## If you already posted something risky

1. Delete it.
2. Tell your program coordinator.
3. Complete any site-required incident steps.
